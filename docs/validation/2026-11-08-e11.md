## Hypotheses

| | Result |
|---|---|
| H1: skills-handoff reaches 100% sooner than skills-shift | **No** (87 s vs 73 s) |
| H2: skills-handoff completes at least as many as one | Yes (9/9 each) |
| H3: skills-handoff reaches 100% sooner than one | Yes (87 s vs 218 s, about 2.5×) |
| H4: skills-multi reaches 100% sooner than one | Yes (140 s vs 218 s, about 1.6×) |
| H5: skills-handoff has a shorter finisher wall time | Yes (28 s vs 103 s) |
| H6: skills-handoff uses fewer total tokens than skills-shift | Yes (0.204M vs 0.423M, about 52% fewer) |

## What it shows

- **The text handoff removes the intended overhead.** The text finisher never called `reference`, against a median of two calls in the picture finisher. Its median finishing time was 28 s against 103 s, and its median finisher tokens were 72% lower. It completed all nine diagrams; the picture finisher completed seven.
- **H1 nevertheless failed.** The picture-shift arm often reached 100% during the bulk phase, so its median `t100` was 73 s. It then kept checking and fixing after that point. Its median total stopping time was 178 s, against 108 s for text handoff. Thus the preregistered first-100% endpoint favours picture shift, while the descriptive stop-time endpoint favours text handoff; these are different measurements and are not interchanged in the hypothesis table.
- **The E10 speed result repeated, with lower reliability.** Twelve cursors with skills reached 100% in a median 140 s against 218 s for one cursor (about 1.6×; E10 was about 1.8×). But two big diagrams stopped at 91%, leaving skills-multi at 7/9 complete (E10: 8/9). Picture shift also completed only 7/9 in this round (E10: 9/9).
- **Text handoff was the most reliable fast arm in this round.** Against one cursor it completed the same 9/9, reached 100% about 2.5× sooner, and used about 1.22× the tokens. Against picture shift it also reduced total stopping time and tokens, without the two incomplete runs.
- **Variance has not disappeared.** Text finishing still ranged from 16 to 133 s, and big-diagram tokens ranged from 0.195M to 0.606M. These are nine repeated diagrams, three per condition, not a new-seed generalization test or a universal effect-size estimate.

A useful next experiment is to repeat text handoff on fresh diagrams and to skip the finisher when the board is already complete. That would test the remaining variance and the gap between first reaching 100% and actually stopping.
