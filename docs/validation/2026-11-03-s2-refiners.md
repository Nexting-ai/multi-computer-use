## Hypotheses

- **H5 (H ≥ B − 1.0): supported** (7.46 vs 7.97).
- **H6 (H tokens ≤ 50% of B): not supported.** H used 6.98M tokens, 162% of B.
- **H7 (H wall time ≤ B): supported** (24.7 vs 54.5 min). B's time is dominated by one 40-minute stall; B finished 15 of 16 buildings by about 18 minutes.
- **Secondary: supported.** H > C (7.46 vs 4.64); objective checks 48/48.

## What this shows

- **Quality needs an agent's attention per building, and that attention costs about the same however it is organised.** A refiner starting from a base used 0.27M tokens, the same as a B agent starting from nothing (0.27M). The cost lies in looking at renders and reasoning over them in a growing context, not in building the first version.
- **The director bounds the run.** H capped every refiner and reviewed all sixteen in one image, so no single building stalled the run (H 24.7 min vs B 54.5). The second round fixed what the review flagged.
- **For open-ended creative work, the harness's token advantage does not hold.** The ~90× savings measured on the web form came from replacing many identical decisions with one. In a city of different buildings, each building needs its own decisions.

## Implications

- The harness's measured strengths are repetitive work across many machines (~90× fewer tokens, one session) and safe, bounded orchestration of many agents (canary, verification, caps, one-look review).
- For creative projects, the remaining cost lever is the per-agent loop itself: smaller screenshots or crops, text feedback instead of full renders, shorter contexts. That is a separate optimisation, not tested here.
